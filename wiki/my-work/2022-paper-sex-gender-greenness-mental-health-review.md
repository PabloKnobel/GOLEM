**Lead.** Systematic review of sex and gender differences in urban greenness and mental health, available online July 16, 2022 in *Health & Place*; Pablo is senior and corresponding author ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 1). Evidence summary: [[fernandeznunez2022gender]].

## How I framed it
- Terminology: studies "either use 'sex' or 'gender' in an indiscriminate or unsystematic way" (p. 1); interchangeable use is problematic because many differences "are often due to social hierarchical structures rather than biological traits" (p. 1).
- Conclusion: women appear to benefit more "but being less likely to use urban greenness than men", possibly due to safety, gender norms and design (p. 8).
- Design call: "urban greenness may unequally distribute mental health benefits in women compared to men" (p. 8).
- Planning line: plan new greenness "with the active voice of female residents especially children" (p. 8).
- Note: Pablo is senior, not first, author; wording reflects the author team.
