**Lead.** Pablo's first- and corresponding-author cohort study of WTC disaster exposure and later residential PM2.5 with glucose and cholesterol in WTC responders; advance access August 16, 2023, issue 2024;193(1) ([[knobel2024association]], p. 87). Evidence summary: [[knobel2024association]].

## How I framed it
- Gap: "no study has evaluated the independent associations of extreme air pollution exposure and subsequent moderate PM2.5 exposure with metabolic biomarkers" (p. 87).
- Climate link: "As extreme air pollution exposure events are becoming more common due to climate change … understanding their implications on cardiometabolic health is essential" (p. 88).
- WTC as an analogue for wildfires: the event "can be conceptually compared with other extreme events featuring high particulate air pollution exposure, like wildfires" (p. 88).
- Public health scale: effects are small but, "considering the ubiquitous nature of PM2.5 and the extent of the population exposed, these differences are of public health concern" (p. 93).
- Policy line: cardiometabolic diseases are not certifiable for WTC healthcare, so "establishing a new link … might substantially affect healthcare costs and accessibility" (p. 94).
