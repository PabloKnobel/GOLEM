**Lead.** Pablo's first-author preprint, posted to medRxiv on August 21, 2026, linking modeled residential bird diversity to frailty deficit accumulation in 20,388 older New Yorkers. Evidence summary: [[knobel2026residential]].

## How I framed it
- Gap: research has "largely focused on the quantity of greenness rather than biodiversity" (abstract, p. 1).
- Policy angle: greenness-focused approaches assume more green area is the lever, "a strategy poorly suited to densely built cities" (p. 2).
- Bird diversity as "a bioindicator of underlying environmental quality shaped by modifiable urban design" (abstract, p. 1).
- Novelty claims: first study of avian biodiversity and a clinical measure of physical aging; time-varying, neighborhood-scale exposure (p. 3); four extensions listed in the discussion (p. 10).
- Life-course window: 65–74 as the period when people are mobile enough to engage with nearby nature but already accumulating deficits (p. 10).
