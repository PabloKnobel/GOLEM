**TL;DR.** A systematic review of 31 randomized controlled trials of green prescriptions (green exercise, horticultural therapy, forest therapy, distraction therapy, park prescription). By vote count, benefits were reported for psychological health and well-being (16 of 24 trials), physical activity (8 of 9), cardiometabolic health (5 of 9) and inflammation (2 of 2); no meta-analysis was done ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 1–2, 11). Pablo is second and corresponding author.

## Key points
- Search: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science to June 30, 2023; 26,176 records, 36 full texts, 31 included (p. 1–2, Fig. 1).
- Meta-analysis not done because outcomes, implementation and measurement tools were not comparable (p. 2).
- Psychological health and well-being: 16 of 24 trials reported benefit, 8 no difference, none harm; forest therapy 7 of 8, horticultural therapy 4 of 6, green exercise 4 of 7 (p. 11) → [[green-prescription--psychological-wellbeing--adewuyi2023health]].
- Cardiometabolic health: 5 of 9 reported benefit; both forest therapy trials positive (p. 11).
- Physical activity: 8 of 9 reported benefit, but in 5 of these both arms received a green prescription and no between-group difference was observed (p. 11) → [[green-prescription--physical-activity--adewuyi2023health]].
- Inflammation: two horticultural therapy trials in community-dwelling adults reported lower inflammatory and T-cell exhaustion biomarkers (p. 11); secondhand.
- No benefit reported for orthopedic outcomes (0 of 2), exhaustion disorder or social engagement; pain 2 of 4 (p. 11, 14).
- Risk of bias: no trial low overall; 28 some concerns, 3 high; blinding and self-reported outcomes were the main issues (p. 12, Fig. 3).
- Older-adult trials are among those summarized (e.g., low-active older adults, nursing home residents, older adults with hypertension) (Table 2, p. 4–10); secondhand.
- Authors' limitations: mostly high-income settings, few no-intervention controls, self-reported outcomes, blinding hard to achieve; they call for objective measures (HRV, accelerometers, cortisol) and attention to age and SES (p. 12–14).
