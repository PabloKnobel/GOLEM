**TL;DR.** An expert workshop report that organizes green space–health pathways into three domains: reducing harm (mitigation: air pollution, heat, noise), restoring capacities (restoration: attention restoration, stress recovery) and building capacities (instoration: physical activity, social cohesion). It gives guidance on pathway-specific exposure metrics, mediation, effect modification and study design ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 301–302, 308, 311).

## Key points
- Three domains, each mapping onto an established perspective on environment and adaptation; they encompass the four conventional pathways (air quality, physical activity, social contacts, stress) and are interrelated (p. 302–303, Fig. 1).
- Adverse effects to weigh: allergenic pollen, disease vectors, crime and fear of crime, green gentrification and displacement (p. 303).
- Air pollution: overall data "do not suggest major impacts of greenspace as means to reduce (i.e. filter) air pollution"; greenspace can raise ozone and biogenic VOCs (p. 303).
- Noise: green barriers, facades and roofs reduce noise by 5–10 dB; field studies consistently showed less noise annoyance with nearby greenspace (p. 304); secondhand.
- Restoration: guided by SRT and [[attention-restoration-theory]]; mediation by recurrent restoration examined in only three studies, two with indications of mediation (p. 304–305). Proposes "greenplace" over "greenspace" (p. 305).
- Physical activity mediation is mixed; presence does not imply use; size, facilities and access matter (p. 305). Social cohesion may mediate mental and general health (p. 305).
- Exposure: NDVI cannot assess quality ("an inaccessible abandoned lot overgrown with vegetation may give the same value as a widely used city park"); Sentinel-2, hyperspectral data, street view with deep learning, and species richness and land cover diversity are proposed (p. 306, 309).
- Buffers in the literature range 30–5,000 m; small (<100 m) buffers for mitigation, larger for physical activity; children and older adults are less mobile, so optimal buffers may differ by group (p. 308). Pathway-specific metrics in Table 1 (p. 308).
- Analysis: multiple-mediator and SEM approaches preferred to Baron–Kenny; cross-sectional mediation is prone to bias (p. 309–310).
- Effect modification: several studies found stronger benefits for low-SES people and deprived areas, others found none; evidence for gender and age is mixed (p. 310); secondhand.
- Recommendations (Table 2): longitudinal, natural-experiment and mover designs; collect quality, access and use; adjust for individual and area SES; report stratified estimates; study LMICs (p. 311).
