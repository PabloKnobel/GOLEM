**TL;DR.** A NeuroView commentary arguing that the physical and social exposome, the [[one-health]] approach and "brain capital" (including "green skills") must be studied and acted on jointly for brain health, with a Box of short- (0–5 y) and long-term (5–15 y) research, policy and technology actions ([[ibanez2024neuroecological]], p. 2, 6, 9–10). No original data.

## Key points
- Physical exposome: air pollutants, heavy metals, plastics (BPA, phthalates) and micro/nanoplastics are described as threats to brain development and neurodegeneration; these are the authors' summaries of others' work (secondhand, p. 3–4).
- Social exposome: aggregate (Gini, HDI) and individual (SDH, SES, education, healthcare, discrimination, violence) factors (p. 4).
- External and internal exposome interact, including via epigenetics (DNA methylation, miRNA), with socially patterned exposure to hazards forming a "vicious circle" (p. 4).
- Children, especially in marginalized communities, framed as most vulnerable (p. 4–5).
- Brain capital: cognitive-emotional resources as an economic asset; "green skills" are "knowledge, abilities, values, and attitudes needed to live in, develop, and support a sustainable and resource-efficient society" (p. 3).
- Research gaps: longitudinal causal studies of specific exposome components, biomonitoring, community engagement and ecological justice, transdisciplinary work (p. 6–7).
- Technology: satellite and remote sensing to reconstruct past exposures, wearables, AI for analysis; "smart" cities with green spaces and pollution control (p. 7, 9–10).
- Policy: the Global Plastics Treaty as an exposome-relevant instrument; integrate the exposome into urban planning and building regulations (p. 5, 9).
