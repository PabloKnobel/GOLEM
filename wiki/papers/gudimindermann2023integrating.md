**TL;DR.** Proposes the Social Exposome: a framework that places the whole social environment, from social interactions to cultural values and structural injustice, inside the exposome, organized by three principles (multidimensionality, reciprocity, timing and continuity) and three transmission pathways (embodiment; resilience and susceptibility or vulnerability; empowerment) ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 1, 7, 11). The framework is not yet empirically validated (p. 16).

## Key points
- Gap: exposome research has focused on biological, chemical and physical exposures; in PubMed to end of 2021, 895 records had "exposome" in title/abstract but only five when combined with "social*" or "socio*" (p. 2). Prior social-domain proposals: socio-exposome (Senier 2017), public health exposome (Juarez 2014), psychoexposome (Colomina 2018), compound exposome (Nwanaji-Enwerem 2021) (p. 2); secondhand.
- Methods: three stages; scoping review found 3,686 deduplicated records, 68 after screening, 14 included; none of the 14 referred to the exposome and only one elaborated a gender perspective (p. 4, 7).
- Dimensions (Fig. 1): innermost **social interactions** (actors, relational dynamics, places), embedded in socioeconomic circumstances and sociodemographic characteristics; systems, institutions and priorities of the political economy; social norms; cultural values (p. 7–9).
- Places include the neighborhood physical environment, explicitly green and blue spaces and parks, as settings of social interaction (p. 9, Table 2).
- **Social inequalities and structural injustices**, with intersectionality and gender, shape the interplay of all dimensions (p. 10).
- Transmission pathways: embodiment (Krieger), resilience and susceptibility or vulnerability (vulnerability as effect modification), empowerment (p. 11–12). Resilience is described as needing periodic *restoration* (Hartig 2004) (p. 12).
- Distinguishes **exposure differentials** (socially patterned exposure) from **differential vulnerability** (social modification of exposure effects) (p. 14).
- Argues against treating social factors only as confounders in exposome analyses (p. 13–14).
- Limitations stated: Global North literature; may be too generic to operationalize; validation planned within Equal-Life (p. 15–16).

## Why it matters for Pablo's work
- Claude's reading: a citable frame for treating neighborhood SES, segregation or social cohesion as part of the urban exposome (not just confounders), and for effect modification by social factors in green space studies ([[green-space-health-pathways]]).
