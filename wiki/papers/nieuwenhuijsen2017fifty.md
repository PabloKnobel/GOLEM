**TL;DR.** A narrative review of 15 reviews concluding that a growing evidence base supports "greener cities are healthier cities", proposing a conceptual framework of mechanisms and modifiers, and calling for longitudinal and intervention designs and standardized green space *quality* assessment ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 63, 65–66, 68–69).

## Key points
- Framework (adapted from Hartig et al. 2014): green space → health through air quality, physical activity, social contacts, stress and restoration; modifiers include distance, accessibility, safety, social and cultural context, gender, age and SES (p. 64–66, Fig. 2).
- Two less-studied mechanisms: the biodiversity hypothesis (microbiota, immune function) and the biogenics hypothesis (p. 67).
- Stress reduction and restoration are described as the most consistent and promising pathways (p. 67).
- Secondhand estimates reported: meta-analysis of 8% lower all-cause and 4% lower cardiovascular mortality, highest vs. lowest green (p. 68); De Vries et al.: stress and social cohesion explained ~20–40% of associations, green physical activity <10% (p. 68). Not yet verified against primary sources.
- Most studies are cross-sectional; SES confounding and selective migration are open concerns (p. 68).
- Research agenda: longitudinal and natural-experiment designs; where, when, how much and what type of green space; **standardized quality assessment** (aesthetics, biodiversity, walkability, facilities, safety, events); susceptible groups (lower SES, women, pregnant women, minorities); green prescriptions (p. 68–70).
