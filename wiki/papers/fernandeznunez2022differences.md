**TL;DR.** A PRISMA review of 22 studies found urban greenness mostly protective for cardiometabolic risk factors, diseases and mortality; benefits were more often larger in women/females for stroke and risk factors such as obesity, and in men/males for cardiovascular disease and CVD mortality ([[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1054, 1061). None of the studies assessed greenness quality (p. 1057). Pablo is senior and corresponding author.

## Key points
- Search of Web of Science, Scopus and PubMed on July 8, 2021; 2,407 records → 58 full texts → 22 included (p. 1055–1057).
- Quality (modified de Keijzer tool): 36.4% very good, 50% good, 13.6% fair (p. 1056–1057).
- Terminology: more than 90% of studies used sex and gender terms interchangeably; only 2 (9.1%) were consistent (p. 1057).
- Exposure: 68.2% assessed residential surrounding greenness, mostly by [[ndvi]] (p. 1057). Studies using NDVI found greenness more protective for females/women in 57.1% (p. 1061).
- No study assessed greenness quality; only one assessed use; 18.2% used more than one indicator (p. 1057) → [[green-space-quality-assessment]].
- Obesity was the most studied outcome (50%); 63.6% of these studies found larger benefits for females/women (p. 1057) → [[urban-greenness--cardiometabolic-health--fernandeznunez2022differences-by-sex-gender]].
- Cardiovascular disease (2 studies) and CVD mortality: benefits larger in males/men; IHD mortality mixed (p. 1060–1061) → [[atherosclerotic-cardiovascular-disease]].
- Proposed explanations: women's greater contact with nearby greenness through care work; men's sports-oriented use; safety concerns limiting women's use; possible late or wrong CVD diagnosis in women (p. 1062–1063).
- Mechanisms read through mitigation, restoration and instoration; NDVI likely captures mitigation, access measures restoration and instoration (p. 1061–1062) → [[green-space-health-pathways]].
- Authors' limitations: English only, inconsistent terminology complicating search (p. 1063). No funding (p. 1064).
- No meta-analysis; results are vote counts by study.
