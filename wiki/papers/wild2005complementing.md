**TL;DR.** The editorial that coined the "exposome": Wild argues that exposure measurement lags far behind genotyping and calls for an exposome "to match the genome", defined at its most complete as all life-course environmental exposures, including lifestyle, from the prenatal period onwards ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848).

## Key points
- Problem: since PCR, effort and money shifted from exposure biomarkers to gene–disease association studies with few reproducible findings; exposure remains "subject to a large degree of misclassification" (p. 1847–1848).
- Definition: "At its most complete, the exposome encompasses life-course environmental exposures (including lifestyle factors), from the prenatal period onwards"; unlike the genome, it is "highly variable and dynamic" (p. 1848).
- Even a partial, targeted exposome is useful (aflatoxin biomarkers and liver cancer as the example); biomarkers must be combined with refined questionnaires (p. 1848).
- Prospective cohorts suit the biomarker approach: repeat sampling and samples taken before disease onset avoid reverse causation (p. 1848).
- Omics (transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics) as possible exposure "fingerprints", still unproven: specificity at low doses, mixtures, temporal instability (p. 1848–1849).
- Call: collaboration across epidemiology, biostatistics, bioinformatics and laboratory and environmental science, and long-term funding for exposure measurement (p. 1849).
- The editorial does not discuss social or built-environment exposures explicitly; it is framed in molecular cancer epidemiology (Claude's reading of the full text).

## Why it matters for Pablo's work
- Claude's reading: the standard origin citation for the exposome and for the life-course framing used in urban exposome work; a later framework argues its social domain was underdeveloped ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 2).
