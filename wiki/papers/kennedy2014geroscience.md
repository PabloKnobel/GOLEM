## Key points
- Geroscience "strives to understand how aging enables chronic disease and seeks to develop novel multi-disease preventative and therapeutic approaches" (p. 709).
- Life expectancy rises but healthspan does not keep pace; older adults live longer with multiple chronic diseases (p. 710).
- Seven pillars (Fig. 1, Table 1): adaptation to stress, epigenetics, inflammation, macromolecular damage, metabolism, proteostasis, stem cells and regeneration; described as "highly intertwined" rather than independent (p. 710–711).
- Goals include using aged and outbred mice, adding mild chronic (including psychological) stress to animal studies, and noting that disease and treatment may accelerate aging (CMV, HIV, childhood chemotherapy) (p. 710–711).
- Environment: aging "also depends on environmental factors, which may be modifiable", but specific environmental influences beyond smoking and obesity "remain poorly understood"; stochasticity is large (p. 712).
- Table 1 lists "link age-related environmental inputs to epigenetic signatures" as an epigenetics goal (p. 711).
- Biomarkers: DNA methylation age predictors are promising, but whether they capture chronologic or biologic age is unclear (p. 712). Inflammaging and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) are highlighted (p. 712).
- Interventions in mice reported as extending lifespan: rapamycin, metformin, acarbose (p. 711–712); secondhand.
