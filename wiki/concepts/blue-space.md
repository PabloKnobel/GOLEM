**Lead.** Blue space is outdoor surface water (rivers, lakes, coast) treated as an urban nature exposure, usually alongside green space. In the vault it appears as one input to Pablo's NYC urban nature profiles ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 2) and as a mapped layer of the European external exposome ([[dehoogh2025europewide]], p. 4).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** exposure · **Also known as:** waterfront, aquatic environments · **Typical measures:** % blue space in a buffer, distance to blue space · **Key sources:** [[knobel2026beyond]], [[dehoogh2025europewide]]

## Definition and measurement
- NYC: % blue space in 300 m buffers from 6-inch LiDAR land cover, one of eight metrics clustered into urban nature profiles ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 2).
- Europe: three distance metrics (to sea-shore, to nearest inland water, minimum of both) from the EU-Hydro dataset, calculated for one time point (2013) on a 100 × 100 m grid ([[dehoogh2025europewide]], p. 4).
- Social Exposome framework: green and blue spaces as places of social interaction in the neighborhood environment ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 9, Table 2).

## Evidence
- The Waterfront profile was associated with lower incident ASCVD versus Low green (HR 0.88; 95% CI 0.81–0.95), as were Park Access and Street Trees ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 3–4) → [[urban-nature-profiles--atherosclerotic-cardiovascular-disease--knobel2026beyond]].
- The IHEN roadmap lists "which types of green and blue space are best for health" as an open research need ([[ihen2026interim]], p. 45).
