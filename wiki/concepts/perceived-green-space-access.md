**Lead.** Residents' own report that usable green or outdoor space is available to them, as opposed to objectively mapped greenness. It may capture access, comfort and safety that satellite measures miss ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 2, 6, 8).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** exposure (perceived) · **Example item:** "Is there a park or other outdoor space in your neighborhood that you are comfortable visiting during the day?" (SEPAHH) · **Key sources:** [[knobel2021associations]]

## Definition and measurement
- Measured by survey; in Philadelphia, as the % of tract adults answering yes to the SEPAHH item above ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 2).
- Correlates weakly with objective measures: 0.32 with tree canopy, 0.21 with vegetation cover, 0.12 with NDVI ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 4).
- The item may include non-green open space, and "comfort" may bundle safety, maintenance, aesthetics and amenities ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 8).

## Evidence
- Highest vs lowest tertile of perceived access: −2.68 percentage points obese, −2.13 high blood pressure, −1.35 diabetes ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 4) → [[perceived-green-space-access--obesity--knobel2021associations]].
- The blood pressure association was restricted to medium- and high-poverty tracts ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 4) → [[perceived-green-space-access--hypertension--knobel2021associations-by-poverty]].
- Perceived quality is prone to subjectivity ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 1).

## Pablo's framing
- [[2021-paper-greenness-cvd-philadelphia]]: perceived and objective metrics as complementary, matched to pathways.

## Open questions
- Reverse causation and self-selection: healthier people may judge spaces as more accessible ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 7).
