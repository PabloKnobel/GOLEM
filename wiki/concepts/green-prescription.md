**Lead.** A nature-based health intervention in which time spent engaging with natural environments is prescribed to benefit health and well-being, typically involving clinicians ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 2). It is the "bringing people to nature" counterpart to greening interventions that bring nature to people ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 1).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** intervention (exposure) · **Formats:** green exercise, horticultural therapy, forest therapy, distraction therapy, park prescription · **Key sources:** [[adewuyi2023health]]

## Definition and measurement
- Formats defined in the review: green exercise (physical activity in green spaces), horticultural therapy (gardening and plant-based activities), forest therapy (sensory immersion in forest), distraction therapy (nature murals in health-care settings), park prescription (using parks and open spaces) ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 12, Table 3).
- Durations in trials ranged from one day to twelve months ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 3).

## Evidence
- In a review of 31 RCTs, benefits were reported for psychological health and well-being in 16 of 24 trials, physical activity in 8 of 9, cardiometabolic health in 5 of 9 and inflammation in 2 of 2; none of the trials had low overall risk of bias ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 11–12) → [[green-prescription--psychological-wellbeing--adewuyi2023health]], [[green-prescription--physical-activity--adewuyi2023health]].
- Comparisons of prescription modes (individual vs group, written vs verbal, with or without pedometer) showed no notable between-group differences ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 12).

## Open questions
- Claude's reading: none of the reviewed trials is described as characterizing the quality of the green setting, so whether quality modifies green prescription effects is open.
- Evidence from low- and middle-income countries, especially Africa, is limited ([[adewuyi2023health]], p. 12).
