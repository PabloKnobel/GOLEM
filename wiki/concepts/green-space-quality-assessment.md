**Lead.** Methods for characterizing the quality of urban green spaces, not only their presence; in-situ observational audits allow objective, standardized characterization ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 2). Pablo's 2019 review of 15 multi-dimensional audit tools is the vault's core source.

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** measure (instrument family) · **Dimensions (checklist):** surroundings, accessibility, facilities, amenities, aesthetics and attractions, incivilities, safety, usage/activities, land covers, policies, animal biodiversity, plant biodiversity · **Key sources:** [[knobel2019systematic]]

## Definition and measurement
- Three approaches: GIS and satellite measures (limited for subtle quality), resident surveys (prone to subjectivity), and direct-observation audits ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 1–2).
- The 15 reviewed tools ranged from 10 to 646 items and 3 to 8 checklist dimensions; per-site time 11–67 min where reported ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 3–5).
- A distinction between "greenness" (any vegetation) and "green space" (structured subset) adds a quality layer ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 2).
- RECITAL: 90 items in 11 dimensions (adding land covers, animal biodiversity and bird biodiversity to classic audit dimensions), scored 0–4 with six scoring methods; usable at item, dimension or overall level ([[knobel2021development]], p. 2, 4) → [[recital]].
- Quality defined (after Gidlow et al. 2018) as attributes that affect the use and interaction of the population with green space: characteristics, features and fitness for purpose ([[knobel2021development]], p. 1).
- A sex/gender review classified greenness exposure as surrounding greenness, greenness quality (site observation, soundscapes, interviews) or self-reported greenness; 7 of 16 studies measured quality ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 2, Table 1).
- In its health application, RECITAL is described with 10 dimensions and 90 items on a five-point Likert scale, summarized as mean and maximum quality of green spaces within 300 m of home ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 2); the dimension count and scale differ from the tool paper (see `[!uncertain]` on [[knobel2021quality]]).

## Evidence
- All reviewed tools showed acceptable inter-rater reliability, but study-unit definitions were weak and fieldwork requirements rarely reported ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 4).
- Biodiversity, land cover, usage, surroundings and policy dimensions were rare (0–4 of 15 tools) ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 5).
- Standardized quality assessment was still lacking in epidemiologic studies in 2017 ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 69).
- In 149 Barcelona green spaces, RECITAL mean item ICC was 0.84; highest reliability in land covers and facilities, lowest in incivilities; dimension alphas ranged from −0.2 (land covers) to 0.8 (aesthetics) ([[knobel2021development]], p. 4–6).
- Dimensions with low alpha (access, facilities, biodiversity) are read as causal (formative) indicators that need not be internally consistent ([[knobel2021development]], p. 6).
- First health application of RECITAL: overall quality was associated with moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, lower overweight/obesity and more green space use; the relevant dimensions differed by outcome ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 6, Table 3) → [[green-space-quality--physical-activity--knobel2021quality]], [[green-space-quality--overweight-obesity--knobel2021quality]], [[green-space-quality--green-space-use--knobel2021quality]].
- Mean quality of nearby green spaces showed generally stronger associations than the single best space ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 6).
- None of 22 greenness–cardiometabolic studies reviewed assessed greenness quality ([[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1057).
- Multidimensional urban nature profiles (park access, street trees, waterfront) were associated with lower ASCVD risk while a high-cover profile was not clearly protective ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 4) → [[urban-nature-profiles--atherosclerotic-cardiovascular-disease--knobel2026beyond]].

## Pablo's framing
- [[2019-paper-green-space-quality-tools-review]]: quality shapes salutogenic potential; calls for clearer definitions, comprehensiveness and reportability.
- [[2021-paper-recital-tool]]: "the most comprehensive tool in terms of the number of quality dimensions included" (p. 5).
- [[2021-paper-green-space-quality-physical-activity-obesity]]: quality explains inconsistent green space–activity findings (p. 2).
- [[2026-paper-urban-nature-profiles-ascvd]]: "beyond greenness" multidimensional metrics.
