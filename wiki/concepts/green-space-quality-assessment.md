**Lead.** Methods for characterizing the quality of urban green spaces, not only their presence; in-situ observational audits allow objective, standardized characterization ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 2). Pablo's 2019 review of 15 multi-dimensional audit tools is the vault's core source.

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** measure (instrument family) · **Dimensions (checklist):** surroundings, accessibility, facilities, amenities, aesthetics and attractions, incivilities, safety, usage/activities, land covers, policies, animal biodiversity, plant biodiversity · **Key sources:** [[knobel2019systematic]]

## Definition and measurement
- Three approaches: GIS and satellite measures (limited for subtle quality), resident surveys (prone to subjectivity), and direct-observation audits ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 1–2).
- The 15 reviewed tools ranged from 10 to 646 items and 3 to 8 checklist dimensions; per-site time 11–67 min where reported ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 3–5).
- A distinction between "greenness" (any vegetation) and "green space" (structured subset) adds a quality layer ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 2).

## Evidence
- All reviewed tools showed acceptable inter-rater reliability, but study-unit definitions were weak and fieldwork requirements rarely reported ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 4).
- Biodiversity, land cover, usage, surroundings and policy dimensions were rare (0–4 of 15 tools) ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 5).
- Standardized quality assessment was still lacking in epidemiologic studies in 2017 ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 69).

## Pablo's framing
- [[2019-paper-green-space-quality-tools-review]]: quality shapes salutogenic potential; calls for clearer definitions, comprehensiveness and reportability.
