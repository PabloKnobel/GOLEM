**Lead.** Whether, and why, environment–health associations differ between women and men. Sex refers to the biological dimension (female/male) and gender to the social one (women/men and the power structures around them); using the terms interchangeably hides differences that are often social rather than biological ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 1).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** method / analytic lens · **Also known as:** sex/gender differences · **Typical analyses:** stratification, interaction terms · **Key sources:** [[fernandeznunez2022gender]], [[knobel2025social]]

## Definition and measurement
- Of 16 greenness–mental health studies, 9 assessed gender only, 4 both and 3 sex only; all binary; none defined the terms and only 7 used them correctly ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 3).
- Observation-based gender assignment can misread gender identity; surveys rarely offer non-binary options ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 6).
- Bolte et al. (2019) propose "sex/gender" to capture their combination (embodiment of gender) (secondhand via [[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 6).

## Evidence
- Urban greenness and mental health: 7 of 16 studies reported larger benefits for women/females, 3 for men/males (all in Iran) ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 3–4) → [[urban-greenness--mental-health--fernandeznunez2022gender-by-sex-gender]].
- Safety is a recurring mediator of women's green space use ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 5, 7).
- Neighborhood social vulnerability and PhenoAgeAccel: +0.27 years per decile in women vs +0.13 in men ([[knobel2025social]], p. 244) → [[social-vulnerability--phenotypic-age-acceleration--knobel2025social-by-sex]].
- Gender and age modification of green space benefits is described as mixed (secondhand via [[markevych2017exploring]], p. 310).
- Urban greenness and cardiometabolic health (22 studies): larger benefits more often in females/women for obesity and risk factors, and in males/men for CVD and CVD mortality; more than 90% of studies used the terms interchangeably ([[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1057, 1061) → [[urban-greenness--cardiometabolic-health--fernandeznunez2022differences-by-sex-gender]].
- Park Access profile and ASCVD: more protective in males (interaction p = 0.04) in older New Yorkers ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 4) → [[urban-nature-profiles--atherosclerotic-cardiovascular-disease--knobel2026beyond-by-sex]].
- Green space quality and overweight/obesity: stronger in females in stratified analyses, without a significant interaction (p > 0.10) ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 5).
- Source-specific PM2.5 and glucose: biomass and motor vehicle PM2.5 associations larger in women, oil combustion in men, in a mostly male WTC cohort ([[krasnov2026association]], p. 499) → [[pm25-sources--glucose--krasnov2026association-by-sex]].
