**Lead.** The set of mechanisms through which contact with green space is thought to affect health, and the factors that modify them. A widely cited version runs from green space through air quality, physical activity, social contacts, stress and restoration to health ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 64–66).

## Definition and measurement
- Framework adapted from Hartig et al. (2014) with mediators and modifiers ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 64–66, Fig. 2).
- Common exposure indicators: surrounding greenness (NDVI or % green in a buffer), access or distance to parks, self-reported use or perception, GPS tracking, quality audits ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 66).
- An alternative organization groups pathways into three domains: reducing harm (mitigation: air pollution, heat, noise), restoring capacities (restoration: attention restoration, stress recovery) and building capacities (instoration: physical activity, social cohesion); the domains encompass the four conventional pathways and interact ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 302–303, Fig. 1).
- Pathway-specific metrics are recommended: presence (tree cover, greenness) suffices for mitigation, while restoration and instoration need perception, use, time spent and quality (type, size, facilities, maintenance) ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 308, Table 1).
- NDVI cannot capture quality: an abandoned overgrown lot can score like a well-used park ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 306).

## Evidence
- Stress reduction and restoration are described as the most consistent pathways ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67).
- Evidence for physical activity is inconsistent, possibly because quality is rarely measured ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67, 69).
- Biodiversity (via microbiota) and biogenics are proposed but little studied ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67).
- One mediation study estimated stress and social cohesion at ~20–40% of the association, and green physical activity at <10%; secondhand via [[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]] (p. 68).
- Bird diversity has been proposed as both a direct experiential exposure and a bioindicator of wider environmental quality ([[knobel2026residential]], p. 3).
- Overall empirical data do not suggest that greenspace substantially filters air pollution, and it can raise some pollutants such as ozone ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 303).
- Mediation by recurrent restoration was tested in only three studies by 2017, two with indications of mediation; physical activity mediation results are mixed (secondhand via [[markevych2017exploring]], p. 305).
- Adverse pathways to weigh: allergenic pollen, disease vectors, crime or fear of crime, and green gentrification ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 303).
- Benefits were stronger for low-SES groups in several studies but not in others; gender and age modification is mixed (secondhand via [[markevych2017exploring]], p. 310).
- A social exposome framework separates exposure differentials from differential vulnerability (social effect modification) as distinct equity mechanisms ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 14).
- Measures should match pathways: presence may suffice for air quality, whereas physical activity requires green space perceived as usable, accessible, inclusive or safe ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 2).
- In Philadelphia, perceived park access was protective mainly in higher-poverty tracts and tree canopy mainly in low-poverty and low % non-Hispanic Black tracts, though without statistical evidence of modification ([[knobel2021associations]], p. 4, 7).
- Quality dimensions (amenities, incivilities, biodiversity, accessibility) are proposed to act on several pathways simultaneously ([[knobel2019systematic]], p. 2).
- Safety: a systematic review reports included studies were unanimous on safety as a mediator between urban greenness and mental health, especially for women, though over half did not measure it ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 7).
- Sex/gender as modifier: 7 of 16 studies showed larger mental health benefits for women/females, 3 for men/males ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 3) → [[urban-greenness--mental-health--fernandeznunez2022gender-by-sex-gender]]; see [[sex-and-gender-effect-modification]].
- Mediation with audited quality: green space use mediated 9.3% (0.8–47%) of the quality–physical activity association; physical activity mediated 9.7% (−1.3–42%, not significant) of quality–overweight/obesity ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 5, Table 4).
- A review mapped NDVI-type exposures mainly to mitigation and access-type exposures to restoration and instoration; only 1 of 22 studies measured use ([[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1057, 1061–1062).

## Open questions
- How much each pathway contributes, and which green space attributes drive each, remain open ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 69).
- Optimal buffer size likely differs by pathway and by population group; children and older adults are less mobile ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 308).
