**Lead.** The set of mechanisms through which contact with green space is thought to affect health, and the factors that modify them. A widely cited version runs from green space through air quality, physical activity, social contacts, stress and restoration to health ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 64–66).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** mechanism framework · **Mediators named:** air quality, temperature, physical activity, social contacts, stress, restoration, biodiversity (microbiota), biogenics · **Modifiers named:** distance, accessibility, safety, social and cultural context, gender, age, SES · **Key sources:** [[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]]

## Definition and measurement
- Framework adapted from Hartig et al. (2014) with mediators and modifiers ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 64–66, Fig. 2).
- Common exposure indicators: surrounding greenness (NDVI or % green in a buffer), access or distance to parks, self-reported use or perception, GPS tracking, quality audits ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 66).

## Evidence
- Stress reduction and restoration are described as the most consistent pathways ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67).
- Evidence for physical activity is inconsistent, possibly because quality is rarely measured ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67, 69).
- Biodiversity (via microbiota) and biogenics are proposed but little studied ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 67).
- One mediation study estimated stress and social cohesion at ~20–40% of the association, and green physical activity at <10%; secondhand via [[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]] (p. 68).
- Bird diversity has been proposed as both a direct experiential exposure and a bioindicator of wider environmental quality ([[knobel2026residential]], p. 3).
