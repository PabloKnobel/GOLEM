**Lead.** The exposome is the totality of non-genetic exposures over the life course, proposed by Wild as the environmental counterpart to the genome to push exposure measurement toward the precision of genotyping ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848). Later work argues the concept has been applied mostly to biological, chemical and physical exposures and has neglected the social domain ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 2).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** theory / research paradigm · **Coined by:** C. P. Wild (2005) · **Typical measures:** exposure biomarkers, omics, questionnaires, geolocation and personal sensing · **Key sources:** [[wild2005complementing]], [[gudimindermann2023integrating]]

## Definition and measurement
- "At its most complete, the exposome encompasses life-course environmental exposures (including lifestyle factors), from the prenatal period onwards"; it is "highly variable and dynamic" ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848).
- Wild saw even a partial, targeted exposome as useful, combining biomarkers with refined questionnaires, ideally in prospective cohorts with repeat sampling ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848).
- Candidate omics tools (transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics) were unproven for exposure assessment in 2005 ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848–1849).
- Later methods named for exposome studies: geolocation, portable and personal sensing, participatory methods, network science ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 13).
- An integrated view splits the external exposome into a social and a physical exposome, with the internal exposome as mediator ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 15, Fig. 3).
- A brain-health framing divides the external exposome into physical (pollution, metals, plastics) and social (aggregate and individual inequality) parts interacting with the internal exposome via epigenetics, and links it to [[one-health]] ([[ibanez2024neuroecological]], p. 3–4).
- A narrative review on aging defines the exposome as "all environmental exposures from conception to death" and splits it into external (environmental) and internal (lifestyle) factors, a different use of "internal" than the biological internal exposome above ([[kareem2025role]], p. S2–S3, Fig. 1).

## Evidence
- Exposome reviews and commentaries far outnumber original exposome studies (Haddad et al. 2019, secondhand via [[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 13).
- Gudi-Mindermann et al. argue that treating social variables only as confounders is problematic in exposome designs, where exposures are interrelated ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 14).
- A noise–dementia meta-analysis calls for exposome approaches to disentangle noise from co-occurring air pollution, heat and road proximity in urban settings ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 8).
- Agnostic exposome-wide analyses may ignore the hierarchy between upstream social variables (race/ethnicity, class) and downstream exposures ([[neufcourt2022assessing]], p. 4); 26 of 73 social-exposome studies gave no formal exposome definition (p. 3).
- A short narrative review (no search strategy) attributes accelerated aging and Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease to air pollution, heavy metals, pesticides, endocrine disruptors, diet and lifestyle via oxidative stress, inflammation and epigenetic change; all claims are secondhand summaries of cited work ([[kareem2025role]], p. S2–S4, Table 1).
- A pan-European external-exposome database (33 factors, 2000–2020) found mostly weak cross-factor correlations (R < 0.4) and stable spatial contrasts over time, but could not harmonise the social or food environment across Europe ([[dehoogh2025europewide]], p. 1–2, 12).
- A 40-country study operationalized a country-level physical, social and sociopolitical exposome (PM2.5, Gini, gender inequality, migration, democracy indices) and linked adverse values to accelerated [[biobehavioral-age-gap]] (d = 0.53); the analysis is ecological ([[hernandez2025exposome]], p. 3090, 3092; Methods).
- The EU-funded IHEN consortium defines the exposome as "the integrated compilation of all the physical, chemical, biological and psychosocial exposure factors, and their interactions, which have an impact on biology and health", and its interim European roadmap sets five research goals (global change, transitions, policy and regulation, medicine, methods and infrastructure). It calls for a long-term European Human Exposome Initiative with large population-based exposome cohorts and FAIR federated data systems; it is an agenda, not evidence ([[ihen2026interim]], p. 6–8, 10, 69).

## Open questions
- Claude's reading: how green space quality and biodiversity metrics fit as components of an urban exposome is not addressed by the sources in the vault so far.
- The IHEN roadmap lists, as research needs, standardized biodiversity metrics (species richness, Shannon/Simpson, functional and genetic diversity) linked to human exposure data, and studies of which types of green and blue space are best for health; it does not answer them ([[ihen2026interim]], p. 17, 45).
