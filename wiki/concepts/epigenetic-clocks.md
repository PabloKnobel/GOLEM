**Lead.** Epigenetic clocks estimate age from DNA methylation; the gap between this estimate and chronological age (epigenetic age acceleration) is used as a measure of biological aging in [[geroscience]] ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 2). Whether methylation age captures chronological or biological age was still open in 2014 ([[kennedy2014geroscience]], p. 712).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** measure (biomarker of aging) · **Also known as:** DNA methylation age, epigenetic age acceleration · **Key sources:** [[crimmins2020social]], [[kennedy2014geroscience]]

## Definition and measurement
- Listed with telomere length and mtDNA copy number as human-study measures of aging hallmarks; downstream summaries include biological age, phenotypic age and pace of aging ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 2).
- Health and Retirement Study 2016 biomarkers include the Horvath epigenetic clock ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 5–7); see [[health-and-retirement-study]].

## Evidence
- In HRS, with age and sex controlled, epigenetic age acceleration was linked to physical functioning and number of diseases; biological measures together explained 6–10% of outcome variance ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 7) → [[biological-aging-measures--age-related-health-outcomes--crimmins2020social]].
- Pablo's NYC EHR study used [[phenotypic-age]] instead and lists "no epigenetic clocks" as a limitation ([[knobel2025social]], p. 246).
