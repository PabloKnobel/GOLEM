**Lead.** Outdoor air temperature as a health exposure, typically averaged over windows before an outcome. In the vault it appears as a co-exposure with air pollution for children's glycemia ([[wu2025air]], p. 1).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** exposure · **Exposure model in vault:** daily 1-km air temperature calibrated from satellite land surface temperature and ground stations (Mexico City) · **Key sources:** [[wu2025air]]

## Definition and measurement
- Daily temperature from a model integrating satellite surface temperature with ground air temperature via land use regression ([[wu2025air]], p. 2).

## Evidence
- −0.626% HbA1c per 1 °C higher 3-month mean temperature in Mexico City children ([[wu2025air]], p. 3) → [[ambient-temperature--hba1c--wu2025air]]; stronger in girls and at ages 8–11 ([[wu2025air]], p. 3).
- The authors describe the wider literature on temperature and glycemia as mixed by climate region and method ([[wu2025air]], p. 5); secondhand.
- Tree canopy offers more shade and thermal comfort than grass or shrubs; secondhand via [[knobel2021associations]] (p. 2).

## Pablo's framing
- [[2025-paper-air-pollution-temperature-hba1c-children]]: context determines direction of temperature associations (p. 5).

## Open questions
- Interactions with PM2.5 were not detected, possibly due to limited power ([[wu2025air]], p. 5).
