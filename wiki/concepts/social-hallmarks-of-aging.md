**Lead.** A set of interrelated social causes of multiple age-related health outcomes (low SES, minority status, adverse life events, adverse psychological states, adverse behaviors), proposed by Crimmins as the social analogue of the geroscience hallmarks of aging ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 1–2).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** exposure set · **Components:** low lifetime SES, minority status, childhood and adult adversity, adverse psychological states, adverse health behaviors · **Key sources:** [[crimmins2020social]]

## Definition and measurement
- Framed with the "fundamental cause" theory (Link and Phelan 1995): low social status links to money, power, connections and knowledge (secondhand via [[crimmins2020social]], p. 2).
- Measured in HRS by childhood and adult SES, race and ethnicity, childhood health and hardship, adult trauma, negative psychological outlook, depression, chronic stressors, obesity, smoking and problem drinking ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 5, Table 1).
- Social factors "get under the skin" through multisystem biology; allostatic load was an early way to measure this ([[crimmins2020social]], p. 2).

## Evidence
- Social variables explained 1.8–6.1 times more outcome variance than biological aging measures → [[social-hallmarks-of-aging--age-related-health-outcomes--crimmins2020social]].
