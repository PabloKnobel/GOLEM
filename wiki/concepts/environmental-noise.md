**Lead.** Unwanted or harmful sound that disrupts communication, sleep, work or leisure; environmental, transportation, occupational and recreational subtypes are distinguished ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 2). Long-term residential noise is a candidate risk factor for dementia ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 1).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** exposure · **Typical metrics:** Lden (24-h weighted), Lnight, dB per 10-dB increment · **Models:** Nord2000, CadnaA, FHWA Traffic Noise Model, kriging · **Key sources:** [[hwang2025comprehensive]]

## Definition and measurement
- Residential exposure is usually modeled: deterministic propagation models (Nord2000, CadnaA, FHWA TNM) or geostatistical kriging; Nord2000 validated to about ±3 dB ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 4–5).
- Common gaps: static traffic data, omitted secondary sources such as construction, and no individual factors such as bedroom placement, insulation or time-activity ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 5).

## Evidence
- Pooled HR for incident ADRD 1.15 (95% CI 1.03–1.28) per 10 dB across six longitudinal studies ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 6–7) → [[environmental-noise--dementia--hwang2025comprehensive]].
- Proposed mechanisms: autonomic and endocrine stress responses, sleep fragmentation, oxidative stress and inflammation ([[hwang2025comprehensive]], p. 2).
- Green barriers, facades and roofs reduce noise by 5–10 dB; secondhand via [[markevych2017exploring]] (p. 304).
