**Lead.** The life-course approach looks at when and how often exposures occur, stressing the cumulative effect of exposures on later health as well as sensitive or critical periods of disease development ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 3). It is built into the original definition of the [[exposome]], which covers exposures "from the prenatal period onwards" ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** theory / framing · **Also known as:** life-course perspective, critical windows, accumulation of risk · **Key sources:** [[wild2005complementing]], [[gudimindermann2023integrating]]

## Definition and measurement
- Wild: "At its most complete, the exposome encompasses life-course environmental exposures (including lifestyle factors), from the prenatal period onwards" ([[wild2005complementing]], p. 1848).
- In the Social Exposome framework, "timing" is one of the systematization categories and the life-course perspective covers cumulative effects and sensitive or critical periods ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 3).

## Evidence
- In the review behind the Social Exposome framework, only 3 of 14 theory-based publications incorporated the life-course perspective in detail ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]], p. 7).
- The IHEN interim roadmap lists life-course epidemiology among research needs on social inequalities; agenda, not evidence ([[ihen2026interim]], p. 23–24).

## Pablo's framing
- Bird-diversity × frailty preprint: ages 65–74 framed as the window when people are mobile enough to engage with nearby nature but already accumulating deficits; see [[2026-preprint-bird-biodiversity-frailty]] and [[bird-biodiversity--frailty-index--knobel2026residential-by-age]].
