**Lead.** How often, and how, people actually visit green spaces — distinct from presence or proximity. Quality is defined by its effect on "the use and interaction of the population" with green space ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 2), and presence does not imply use ([[markevych2017exploring]], p. 305).

> [!info] Infobox
> **Kind:** outcome / mediator · **Typical measures:** self-reported days per week in green spaces; observation · **Key sources:** [[knobel2021quality]]

## Definition and measurement
- Barcelona Health Survey: days per week of free time spent in parks, gardens or other green spaces (0–7), used as a proxy of use ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 3).
- Among 22 greenness–cardiometabolic studies, only one assessed use ([[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1057).

## Evidence
- Higher audited quality within 300 m: ERC 1.08 (1.01–1.15) more days of use per IQR ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 6) → [[green-space-quality--green-space-use--knobel2021quality]].
- Bird biodiversity, access, aesthetics, safety and potential use dimensions were associated with use ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 5) → [[bird-biodiversity--green-space-use--knobel2021quality]].
- Use mediated 9.3% of the quality–physical activity association, but not quality–overweight/obesity ([[knobel2021quality]], p. 5, Table 4).
- Gendered use: women may avoid green spaces perceived as unsafe and use them along care roles; men more often for sport (secondhand via [[fernandeznunez2022differences]], p. 1062–1063).
