**Finding.** Urban ZIP codes with a higher share of non-Hispanic Black residents had higher total PM2.5 and higher Heavy Fuel Oil and Industrial, Metal Processing Industry and Agricultural, and Motor Vehicle PM2.5, but lower Coal and Oil Combustion and Biomass Burning PM2.5 ([[knobel2023socioeconomic]], p. 3).

> [!todo] Effect sizes are only shown graphically (Fig. 3) and in Supplementary Table 2; not available in the converted text.

## Context
- Adjusted for population density, RUCA, % over 65 and % female (p. 3).
- Heavy Fuel Oil associations with % Black strongest in the West, then Northeast (p. 3).
- Authors relate patterns to redlining and highway siting (p. 2, 5).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Authors report consistency with Bell and Ebisu 2012 for V, EC, OC and Zn (secondhand via [[knobel2023socioeconomic]], p. 5).
