**Finding.** Glucose associations with biomass-burning (+1.08%; 0.32–1.85) and motor vehicle/resuspended dust PM2.5 (+1.34%; 0.76–1.93) were larger in women, and with oil-combustion PM2.5 (+0.68%; 0.03–1.34) in men ([[krasnov2026association]], p. 499).

## Context
- Corresponding estimates in the other sex: men biomass −0.45%, MVRD −0.04%; women oil −0.18%, all null (p. 499).
- Women were 13.38% of the cohort (p. 500, Table 1).
- Authors suggest behavior, physiology, pregnancy-related metabolic change and higher PTSD/depression in women; men's oil association may reflect occupational exposure patterns (p. 501).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Same cohort: PM2.5–glucose was larger in people with diabetes ([[pm25--glucose--knobel2024association-by-diabetes]]), another susceptibility pattern.
