**Finding.** Compared with a Low-green profile, Waterfront (HR 0.88; 0.81–0.95), Park Access (0.89; 0.84–0.94) and Street Trees (0.92; 0.87–0.98) profiles had lower incident ASCVD risk, while High Cover was not clearly protective (0.95; 0.88–1.02) in older New Yorkers ([[knobel2026beyond]], p. 4, Table 2).

## Context
- Adjusted for age, sex, race (White/non-White), insurance, tract % poverty and % non-Hispanic Black (p. 3).
- Similar direction when adjusting for CDC Environmental Justice Index domains instead (p. 4, Table S4).
- Exposures measured once and imputed across years; median follow-up 3.8 y (p. 1–2).
- Authors read High Cover as the NDVI-like profile: "increased nature cover ... might not be the main driver" (p. 4).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Consistent with [[knobel2021associations]], where perceived access and tree canopy, not NDVI, were associated with cardiometabolic risk factors (cited by the authors, p. 4).
