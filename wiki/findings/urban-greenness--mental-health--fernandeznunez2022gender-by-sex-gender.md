**Finding.** Of 16 studies on sex or gender differences in the urban greenness–mental health association, 7 reported larger benefits for women/females, 3 for men/males, and 4 had no clear beneficiary; one found no sex difference and one minimal differences ([[fernandeznunez2022gender]], p. 3).

> [!uncertain] The review also states that all studies found at least one difference, which conflicts with the no-difference study (p. 3).

## Context
- The three studies favoring men/males were in Iran and pointed to safety and socio-cultural constraints on women (p. 3–4).
- Most studies were cross-sectional (11 of 16) and only 7 used sex/gender terms correctly (p. 2–3).
- Not pooled; the vote count mixes depression, stress, suicide and social-health outcomes (p. 3–5).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Gender and age are listed as modifiers in the main pathway framework ([[nieuwenhuijsen2017fifty]], p. 64–66); evidence there described as mixed (secondhand via [[markevych2017exploring]], p. 310).
