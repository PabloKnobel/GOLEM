**Finding.** Across 2,211 healthy controls and people with AD or FTLD in six Latin American countries, a more adverse multidimensional social exposome was associated with worse cognition (β 0.261, 95% CI 0.18–0.282), functional ability (β 0.079) and neuropsychiatric symptoms (β 0.105), and the global score explained more than single dimensions combined via Lasso (cognition common effect 0.23 vs 0.12) ([[migeot2025social]], p. 3–4, 6).

## Context
- Cross-sectional; clinical, non-representative sample; self-reported lifespan exposures (p. 10–11).
- Model fit, whole sample: CFI 0.939, TLI 0.893, RMSEA 0.076, SRMR 0.049 (p. 3).
- Robust to age, sex, disease severity, country, and excluding education from the MSE (p. 6).
- Scaling direction is reported inconsistently in the paper (see [[migeot2025social]]).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Empirical counterpart of the conceptual [[social-exposome]] framework ([[gudimindermann2023integrating]]); like [[social-vulnerability--phenotypic-age-acceleration--knobel2025social]], it treats social exposures as a combined mixture rather than single confounders — different outcome and area-level vs individual-level measures.
