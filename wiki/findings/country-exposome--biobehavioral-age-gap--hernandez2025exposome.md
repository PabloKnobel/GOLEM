**Finding.** Across 40 countries, people living where country-level exposome conditions were worse (higher PM2.5, inequality and gender inequality, more migration, weaker democracy indicators) had larger biobehavioral age gaps (combined d = 0.53). The associations held after adjusting for individual SES and were stronger in women ([[hernandez2025exposome]], p. 3090, 3092, 3094).

## Context
- Exposures are national indicators assigned by country and year of interview. There is no within-country variation, so the analysis is ecological and confounded by everything else that differs between countries.
- Effect sizes are reported as Cohen's d derived from regressions. Per-factor coefficients for PM2.5 are given only in figures (Fig. 2g).
- Cross-sectional (p. 3097–3098).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Extends the same group's individual-level social-exposome work ([[social-exposome--dementia--migeot2025social]]) to country-level indicators. In contrast, [[social-vulnerability--phenotypic-age-acceleration--knobel2025social]] uses small-area exposure and a biomarker clock.
