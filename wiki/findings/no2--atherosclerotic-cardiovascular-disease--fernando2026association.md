**Finding.** In a nationwide Israeli case-crossover study, an 8-ppb increase in NO2 summed over lag days 0–5 was associated with higher odds of ischemic stroke or TIA (OR 1.057, 95% CI 1.034–1.081), adjusting for PM2.5 and temperature ([[fernando2026association]], p. 1, 4).

## Context
- Adjusted for PM2.5 over the same window and temperature lag 0–4; case-crossover controls time-invariant confounders (p. 3–4).
- Peak on lag days 3–5; lag 1 OR 0.976 (0.961–0.994), lag 5 OR 1.042 (1.022–1.063) (p. 4–5).
- Robust to 3–6-day windows (1.053–1.057), excluding recurrent events (1.061), same-day PM2.5 (1.065) and ischemic stroke only (1.042) (p. 5, 7).
- PM2.5 not associated in the same model (OR 0.997 per 10 µg/m³) (p. 7).

## How it relates to other evidence
- Contrasts with NO2 not associated with child HbA1c in Mexico City ([[no2--hba1c--wu2025air]]) — different outcome, population and time scale, not a contradiction.
