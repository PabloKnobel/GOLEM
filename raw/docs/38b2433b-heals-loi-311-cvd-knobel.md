**PROJECT SUMMARY**

**Summary (2–3 sentences describing the project)**

Every day, New Yorkers file thousands of 311 complaints. These records are a free by-product of city management and offer a continuously updated, address- and day-level map of urban stressors. They capture what residents actually experience and perceive, which objective monitoring often misses. We will turn 311 complaints into chronic (yearly) and acute (daily) exposure metrics and link them to Mount Sinai health records to test whether these stressors increase cardiovascular disease risk and trigger acute cardiovascular events. The resulting open, scalable tool can be applied in any city with a 311 system, adds new layers to the PI’s pending NIA R01 on the urban exposome, and the potential evidence will lay the foundation for an R01 on perceived and objective urban stressors and health. This is a resubmission of a P30 pilot.

**Specific Aims (brief statement of aims/objectives)**

**Aim 1. Chronic effects:** Determine whether long-term (yearly) exposure to noise, housing-condition (e.g., loss of heat), and rodent complaints is associated with incident cardiovascular disease (myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure) among adult Mount Sinai patients.

**Aim 2. Acute effects:** Determine whether day-to-day spikes in these complaints near the home trigger cardiovascular emergency department visits and hospital admissions, using a case-crossover design.
